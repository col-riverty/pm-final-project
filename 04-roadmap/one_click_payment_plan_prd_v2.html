1. The Big Picture

Vision

Enable consumers with stable income and an opened case to create a clear, affordable payment plan in one mobile-first session without abandoning the resolution flow or contacting support.

Press Release

Consumers can now resolve an opened case by selecting an eligible payment plan, reviewing the complete repayment schedule, and confirming the agreement in one streamlined digital flow. Clear installment amounts, due dates, and total repayment information remove the uncertainty that currently causes consumers to leave before completing a resolution.

The One-Click Payment Plan Setup targets the drop from 49% starting a self-service flow to only 31% successfully resolving an issue. By keeping eligibility, plan selection, confirmation, and success feedback in one session, the experience aims to increase the Digital Resolution Rate while protecting the existing mobile consumer base.

2. The Details

User Stories

US1: Determine eligibility

As a consumer with stable income and an opened case, I want to know immediately whether I am eligible for a payment plan, so that I do not spend time entering a flow I cannot complete.

Acceptance criteria

  • Eligibility is evaluated when the payment-plan entry point is selected.
  • An eligible consumer reaches the plan-selection screen without entering information already associated with the case.
  • An ineligible consumer receives a clear reason and an available next action.
  • The interface does not display a selectable plan that the consumer cannot confirm.
  • Eligibility messaging avoids legal or technical terminology.

US2: Select and understand a payment plan

As a consumer who qualifies for a payment plan, I want to compare the available installment options and understand their consequences, so that I can choose an affordable schedule with confidence.

Acceptance criteria

  • Every option displays the installment amount, payment frequency, first due date, number of installments, and total repayment.
  • The selected option is visually distinct from unselected options.
  • The repayment summary updates immediately when the consumer changes the selection.
  • No fee, amount, or due date is hidden behind expandable content.
  • The prototype prevents confirmation until one valid option has been selected.

US3: Confirm the agreement

As a consumer who has selected an affordable payment plan, I want to confirm it without offline steps or repeated data entry, so that I can resolve my case in the same session.

Acceptance criteria

  • The consumer can confirm the selected plan from the plan-selection screen.
  • Confirmation requires one explicit action after the final summary is visible.
  • The confirmation state cannot be triggered accidentally by scrolling or navigation.
  • A successful confirmation displays the next payment date, installment amount, and reference number.
  • A failed confirmation preserves the selected plan and provides a retry path.
  • No successful state is displayed unless the prototype receives its predefined success condition.

Screens to Build

Screen 1: Entry Point

Confirm case context and move an eligible consumer directly into payment-plan selection.

  • Page title: Resolve your case
  • Case reference
  • Outstanding balance
  • Case-status indicator
  • Short payment-plan description
  • Eligibility-status message
  • Primary CTA: Set up a payment plan
  • Secondary action: View other options
  • Brief notice explaining that available plans depend on case eligibility
  • Mobile navigation header
  • Loading state for eligibility evaluation
  • Ineligible-state message with a next action

Screen 2: Feature Core

Allow the consumer to select, understand, and confirm a payment plan.

  • Page title: Choose your payment plan
  • Progress label: Step 1 of 2
  • Two or three selectable installment-plan cards
  • Installment amount
  • Payment frequency
  • First payment date
  • Number of installments
  • Total repayment amount
  • Selected-plan indicator
  • Real-time payment summary
  • Required agreement checkbox
  • Primary CTA: Confirm payment plan
  • Secondary action: Back
  • Inline validation message
  • Processing state after confirmation
  • Plain-language error banner with Try again action

Screen 3: Success and Confirmation

Remove uncertainty after confirmation and make the next required action explicit.

  • Success indicator
  • Page title: Your payment plan is active
  • Confirmation reference number

  • Payment frequency
  • Next payment date
  • Total repayment amount

  • Confirmation-state identifier for prototype evaluation

Functional Requirements

FR1: Eligibility gating

The system must classify the prototype consumer as eligible or ineligible before presenting selectable payment plans.

FR2: Plan availability

An eligible consumer must be shown between two and three predefined payment-plan options. Each option must include the installment amount, frequency, first due date, number of installments, and total repayment.

FR3: Complete cost visibility

The total repayment shown in the summary must equal the sum of all displayed installments. No additional amount may appear after confirmation.

FR4: Immediate summary update

When the consumer selects a different plan, the repayment summary must update within 300 milliseconds in the prototype.

FR5: Confirmation control

The confirmation button must remain disabled until a valid payment plan is selected, the required agreement checkbox is selected, and all displayed plan data passes predefined validation.

FR6: Single-session completion

An eligible consumer must be able to move from the entry point to successful confirmation without leaving the three-screen experience or repeating case information.

FR7: Error recovery

If confirmation fails, the interface must preserve the selected plan and agreement state, explain the failure in plain language, and offer a retry action on the same screen.

FR8: Mobile usability

All primary content and actions must remain usable at a 375-pixel viewport width without horizontal scrolling. The primary CTA must remain visible or reachable without ambiguous navigation.

Technical Constraints

Build within these constraints

  • Use React functional components.
  • Use local component state with useState only.
  • Use predefined local mock data for the case, eligibility, payment plans, errors, and confirmation states.
  • Implement exactly three user-facing screens.
  • Simulate loading, success, ineligible, validation-error, and confirmation-error states locally.
  • Keep all calculations deterministic and derived from local fixture data.

Do not build

  • No external APIs.
  • No backend service.
  • No authentication or login.
  • No database or persistent storage.
  • No real payment processing.
  • No actual contractual agreement creation.
  • No AI or generative functionality.
  • No external state-management library.
  • No routing framework unless required by the prototype environment.
  • No analytics integration.
  • No document upload.
  • No support-agent integration.
  • No production eligibility or affordability decision engine.

Edge Cases

Eligibility unavailable

If eligibility cannot be determined from the mock state, the prototype must show a neutral unavailable state and must not display estimated plan options.

No available payment plans

If the consumer is eligible in principle but no plan options are available, the entry screen must explain that a payment plan cannot currently be configured and present View other options.

Invalid repayment data

If an installment amount, due date, number of installments, or total repayment value is missing or inconsistent, confirmation must be blocked.

Consumer selects no plan

The confirmation CTA remains disabled, and the interface identifies plan selection as the required next action.

Agreement not accepted

The confirmation CTA remains disabled, and the required checkbox is visually associated with the blocking validation message.

Confirmation fails

The consumer remains on the feature-core screen. The selected option and agreement state remain intact, and a retry action is shown.

Repeated confirmation

The CTA enters a processing state after the first selection and rejects duplicate confirmation actions.

Small-screen layout

At a 375-pixel viewport, plan cards must stack vertically and all monetary values, due dates, labels, and actions must remain readable.

Long or localized values

Reference numbers, currency values, and date labels must wrap or truncate safely without covering another UI element.

Safety and hallucination guard

The prototype must never generate, infer, or modify eligibility, installment amounts, fees, due dates, or contractual wording. It must display only predefined fixture values. If required data is unavailable or inconsistent, the system must block confirmation and show an error state.

Decision Log

Decision 1: Payment-plan completion over broad resolution guidance

Decision: Build the direct payment-plan path rather than the full Guided Resolution Wizard.

Reason: The selected persona has stable income and an opened case. A direct payment arrangement is the shortest path to the primary metric, while a broader wizard adds screens and decision points that may increase abandonment.

Scope protected: Exactly three screens, one resolution outcome, and no alternate end-to-end resolution journeys.

Decision 2: Deterministic fixture data over AI explanation

Decision: Use predefined case and payment-plan data rather than AI-generated explanations or recommendations.

Reason: The prototype must test whether reduced transactional friction improves completion. Introducing AI would add complexity, create safety risks around financial and contractual content, and make the test results harder to attribute to the payment-plan flow.

Scope protected: No external APIs, no generated financial information, no claim explanation, and no recommendation model.

Evals

Eval 1: Flow accuracy

Target: At least 95% of test runs must preserve the correct selected plan, installment amount, first due date, total repayment, and confirmation reference across the feature-core and success screens.

Failure condition / observation: Any mismatch between the selected plan and the confirmation screen, or any invented value.

Eval 2: Time on task

Target: At least 80% of eligible participants must complete the payment-plan setup in three minutes or less without moderator assistance.

Failure condition / observation: No more than 25% of participants should abandon the prototype before the success screen.

Eval 3: Safety triggers

Target: 100% of tests containing missing, inconsistent, or unavailable financial data must block confirmation and show an appropriate error state.

Failure condition / observation: Zero tolerance: invented installment amounts, changed repayment totals, unsupported eligibility claims, false success confirmation, hidden or omitted repayment information, or confirmation despite invalid fixture data.
